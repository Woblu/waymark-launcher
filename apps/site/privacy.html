Waymark Launcher

Privacy policy

Last updated 12 November 2026.

In short

Waymark Launcher has no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and it doesn't send anything about you or your computer to us. It connects to the services on this page to do what you ask, and it checks for new versions of itself, which you can turn off.

Every connection shows your computer's internet address to the service on the other end, the same as visiting a website.

Updates to Waymark Launcher

Twenty seconds after it starts, and every six hours after that, Waymark Launcher asks waymark-launcher.vercel.app for the newest version, which points to the release on GitHub. If you turned on Get beta versions early, it asks GitHub's API for the list of releases instead. Neither request carries an account or an identifier. Keep Waymark Launcher up to date in Settings turns both off. See Vercel's privacy policy and GitHub's.

Signing in to Minecraft

Waymark Launcher talks to Microsoft, Xbox Live and Mojang to confirm you own the game when you sign in, when you play, when you change your skin, and when it checks that the game still starts after an update. Your Microsoft password is typed on Microsoft's own page, never in Waymark Launcher. Waymark Launcher keeps your Minecraft username, account id and skin address on your computer, and a sign-in token in your system's keychain. See Microsoft's privacy statement.

Downloading the game, mods and packs

The game, Java and the default skins come from Mojang. Mods, packs, shaders and resource packs come from the store you choose: Modrinth, CurseForge, Feed The Beast, ATLauncher or Technic. Those stores see your searches. Modrinth packs can also name files on GitHub or GitLab, which their format allows. Technic packs download from wherever their author put them, often Dropbox.

To show the right page for a mod you added by hand, Waymark Launcher sends a fingerprint of the file to Modrinth. A fingerprint is a short code worked out from the file, and the file itself stays on your computer.

Mod loaders come from their own publishers: Fabric, Quilt, Legacy Fabric, Babric, Ornithe, Forge, NeoForge, LiteLoader, and Rift and Cleanroom through GitHub. A few old loaders need files their publishers no longer host. Rift's come from Maven Central and the Internet Archive, and the libraries old Forge downloads at startup come from Prism Launcher's file server.

A speedrunning preset reads speedrun.com's list of allowed mods from GitHub, then downloads those mods from the GitHub and Modrinth addresses the list gives.

Project pages show the pictures and videos their authors added, so those load from wherever the author hosts them.

If you paste a link into the cape maker, Waymark Launcher downloads the picture from that address. If the link is to a page, like a GIF on Tenor or Giphy, it also downloads the picture the page offers for previews.

Builds of Minecraft from before 1.6 come from Mojang, Omniarchive and BetaCraft, LegacyFix comes from GitHub, and the libraries it needs from Legacy Fabric and Maven Central. While a version runs with LegacyFix, LegacyFix talks to Mojang to sign you in to servers and to load skins, and can fetch that version's sounds from Mojang or BetaCraft. It's set to keep Classic saves on your computer and to ask no other server for skins.

Friends

Friends are optional and off until you make a Waymark Launcher account. That account is a username and a password with no email address, kept on a database run by Supabase. It stores your username, a hash of your password, your friends list, anything a friend sends you, when you were last online and the name of the instance you're playing.

To limit sign-ups to five an hour from one internet address, the server keeps a keyed hash of the address each new account came from. The key is secret, so the hash can't be turned back into the address. A hash is deleted at the first sign-up after it turns a day old.

If you host a server and turn on Let friends join, Waymark Launcher asks your router to open the server's port while it runs, and your friends can see your internet address and your instance's setup. Turning it off, or stopping the server, takes that back.

You can hide your status, block people, or delete the account and everything above from the app.

Discord

Discord rich presence is off until you turn on Show what you're playing on your Discord profile in Settings. Then, while a game runs, Waymark Launcher tells the Discord app on your computer the instance's name, its Minecraft version and loader, and the server's name when you joined it from the server list. Discord shows that on your profile. A private server's address is left out. See Discord's privacy policy.

Only when you ask

  • Sharing a log. Waymark Launcher uploads it to mclo.gs, after taking out folder names and sign-in tokens.
  • Servers in your list. Waymark Launcher asks each one for its player count and description.
  • The server list. Browsing it searches Modrinth, where server owners list their servers. Opening a server's page asks that server for its status, and joining one connects to it.
  • Links you open. They open in your web browser.

On your computer

Your instances, waymarks, worlds, screenshots, skins and settings stay on your computer. Crash reports are read there and aren't sent anywhere.

To offer imports, Waymark Launcher reads the instance lists of other launchers on your computer: Prism Launcher, MultiMC, ATLauncher, GDLauncher, the CurseForge app, the Modrinth App and the Minecraft Launcher. What it reads isn't sent anywhere. Importing from the Minecraft Launcher copies your worlds, mods and settings, and never its sign-in files.

Contact
